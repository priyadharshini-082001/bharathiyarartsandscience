About the Department

Inspirational Thought

"Without your involvement you can't succeed; with your involvement you can't fail."

– Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Established in 2003, the Department of Physics offers undergraduate and postgraduate programmes to provide students with a strong foundation in physical sciences. The department further expanded its academic programmes with the introduction of the M.Phil. programme during the academic year 2014–2015.

The department is supported by 10 dedicated faculty members and 2 laboratory assistants. Our highly qualified faculty members are actively involved in teaching, research and academic development, providing students with quality education and individual guidance.

Physics is the foundation of most modern technologies. Studying physics develops quantitative reasoning, analytical abilities and problem-solving skills that are valuable across engineering and non-engineering disciplines. A major in Physics provides students with a strong intellectual foundation and prepares them for diverse career opportunities in scientific, technical and research-oriented fields.

Academic Excellence

The department promotes conceptual understanding through theoretical instruction, practical demonstrations and experimental learning. Students explore important areas such as mechanics, optics, spectroscopy, electronics, electricity and magnetism, thermodynamics, molecular physics and modern physics.

Apart from core Physics programmes, the department also offers Allied and Elective Physics courses to students from other science departments, particularly Chemistry and Mathematics. This interdisciplinary approach encourages students to understand the applications of physics in different scientific fields.

Programmes and Learning Opportunities

The department provides undergraduate and postgraduate learning opportunities through B.Sc. Physics and M.Sc. Physics programmes. Students develop theoretical knowledge, practical competence, research awareness and technical skills through a balanced academic curriculum.

Co-Curricular and Extracurricular Activities

The department regularly organizes workshops, state-level seminars and national-level seminars to expose students to recent scientific developments. National Science Day is celebrated grandly every year on February 28th to promote scientific awareness, innovation and research interest among students.

Along with academic excellence, students are encouraged to participate in curricular, co-curricular and extracurricular activities. These opportunities help identify and nurture their hidden talents, creativity, leadership qualities and confidence.

Department Vision

Our Vision

To motivate women learners to become competent physics professionals and lifelong scientific thinkers.

Our Commitment

The department aspires to develop scientifically informed, confident and capable graduates through quality education, practical experimentation and research-oriented learning. Students are encouraged to question, investigate and understand the physical world while applying scientific principles to technological developments and real-world challenges.

Department Mission

Our Mission

  • Teach physics through clear concepts, experiments and problem-solving.
  • Use innovative methods to connect theory with practical applications.
  • Build laboratory, analytical and research capabilities.
  • Prepare students for advanced study, education and technology-oriented careers.
  • Encourage scientific curiosity, creativity and interdisciplinary learning.

Learning Through Scientific Exploration

The department integrates theoretical instruction with laboratory experiments, demonstrations and analytical activities. Students are encouraged to develop logical reasoning, quantitative skills, experimental accuracy and independent thinking. The learning environment supports scientific investigation and prepares students to apply physics knowledge in academic, research and technology-oriented settings.

Laboratory Infrastructure

The Department of Physics has well-equipped laboratories designed to provide students with practical exposure and hands-on experience in various areas of physics. The laboratories are furnished with modern and high-quality apparatus that help students understand scientific principles through experimentation.

Experiments are conducted under the guidance of well-trained faculty members. Appropriate experimental conditions are maintained to ensure accuracy and reliability. Special attention is given to controlling environmental conditions, particularly lighting arrangements for optical experiments, using suitable laboratory facilities.

Laboratory Specializations

The Physics laboratories are classified into different practical and experimental areas to provide comprehensive exposure to undergraduate and postgraduate students.

  • Mechanics
  • Optics and Spectroscopy
  • Electronics and Digital Electronics
  • Properties of Matter and Sound
  • Thermodynamics
  • Electricity and Magnetism
  • Molecular Physics
  • Solid State Physics
  • Optical Physics and Lasers
  • Microprocessors and Microcontrollers
  • Waves and Power Supplies

Major Experiments and Practical Facilities

Students perform a wide range of experiments to strengthen their understanding of theoretical concepts and develop technical and analytical laboratory skills.

  • Bifilar Pendulum
  • Zener Diode in Forward and Reverse Bias
  • Newton's Rings

  • Calibration of Ammeter

Hands-on Experimental Learning

Through these practical sessions, students gain experience in handling scientific instruments, recording observations, analysing experimental results and understanding the practical applications of physics. The laboratory environment encourages precision, scientific investigation, teamwork and independent learning.

Programmes Offered

The Department of Physics provides undergraduate and postgraduate education in physical sciences. The programmes combine conceptual understanding, mathematical analysis and experimental practice to develop a strong foundation for scientific study and professional growth.

  • B.Sc. Physics
  • M.Sc. Physics

Academic Pathway

Students can begin with undergraduate study in physics and progress to postgraduate specialisation. The programmes support further learning in physics, electronics, materials science and applied sciences, along with opportunities in teaching, laboratory work, instrumentation and research, subject to applicable eligibility requirements.

B.Sc. Physics

About the Course

A three-year undergraduate programme covering mechanics, electricity, magnetism, optics, electronics, thermal physics, quantum concepts and practical measurement. The programme builds scientific reasoning and experimental accuracy through theoretical study, laboratory exercises and problem-solving activities.

Learning Focus

  • Mechanics, waves, optics and thermal physics
  • Electricity, magnetism and electronics
  • Modern, atomic and quantum physics
  • Laboratory measurement, instrumentation and computation

Career Opportunities

  • Laboratory or instrumentation assistant
  • Electronics, energy, optics and technical-support roles
  • Quality testing and measurement services
  • Teaching after the required professional qualification
  • Scientific and technical competitive examinations
  • M.Sc. Physics, electronics, materials or applied science

M.Sc. Physics

About the Course

A postgraduate programme that advances theoretical, experimental and applied physics. Students strengthen mathematical analysis, instrumentation, research practice and the ability to investigate complex physical systems. The programme develops advanced subject knowledge and supports further academic, scientific and technology-oriented pursuits.

Learning Focus

  • Classical, quantum and statistical physics
  • Electromagnetism, electronics and solid-state physics
  • Instrumentation, computational methods and advanced experiments
  • Research methodology, seminars and project work

Career Opportunities

  • Research assistant or project associate
  • Instrumentation, electronics and testing roles
  • Scientific laboratories and technical organizations
  • Teaching after meeting applicable eligibility norms
  • Data and computational roles with additional skills
  • Ph.D. and advanced research in physics or materials science
